# Course Syllabus: The Game Design & Programming Odyssey
**Target Audience:** High-performing 11-12 year olds  
**Duration:** 8 Weeks (1-hour sessions + independent time)  
**Core Philosophy:** Move from "Coder" (writing syntax) to "Designer" (creating agency, narrative, and experience).

---

## 🎯 Course Objective
Students will not just learn to write code; they will learn to design systems. By the end of the course, every student will have moved a concept from a **paper prototype** to a **functional, "juicy" digital game** with a cohesive story and unique mechanics.

---

## 🛠 The Pedagogical Framework

### 1. The "Designer > Coder" Approach
We teach programming as a tool to solve design problems. We don't teach "Variables"; we teach "Game State." We don't teach "If-Statements"; we teach "Player Choices."

### 2. The Side-Quest System (For High Performers)
To prevent boredom, every standard task comes with optional "Side-Quests":
*   **Standard Task:** Make the player move.
*   **Side-Quest (Physics):** Add momentum and friction so the movement feels "weighty."
*   **Side-Quest (Visual):** Make the player trail particles when they move quickly.

### 3. The "Juice" Factor
We emphasize "Game Juice"—the visual and auditory feedback (screen shake, sound effects, particle bursts) that turns a boring interaction into an addictive experience.

---

## 📅 The 8-Week Curriculum

### Phase 1: The Blueprint (Design & Logic)
*Focus: Moving from "idea" to "structured plan" using non-digital tools.*

| Week | Theme | Key Activity | Design Concept |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **The Skill Audit** | **The Player Stat Sheet:** Students map their own strengths (Logic, Art, Story, Sound) to define their "Designer Persona." | **Paper Prototyping:** Creating "Mechanic Cards" and testing movement on a physical grid. |
| **2** | **The Narrative Thread** | **Flowcharting the Journey:** Mapping out how a player moves from Start $\rightarrow$ Conflict $\rightarrow$ Victory. | **Mechanics vs. Theme:** Distinguishing between an "action" (jumping) and a "story" (leaping over lava). |

### Phase 2: The Translation (Implementation)
*Focus: Using text-based code (Python/Replit) to bring paper designs to life.*

| Week | Theme | Key Activity | Technical Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **3** | **The Digital Skeleton** | **The Core Loop:** Implementing the "Input $\rightarrow$ Update $\rightarrow$ Draw" cycle. | The Game Loop & Basic Movement. |
| **4** | **Conflict & Stakes** | **The Lose State:** Building obstacles that interact with the player. | Collision Detection & Boolean Logic. |
| **5** | **Economy & Systems** | **The Expansion:** Adding variables like Score, Health, or Mana. | Variables, Arithmetic, and State Management. |

### Phase 3: The Polish (Aesthetics & Iteration)
*Focus: Turning a "working" game into a "professional" game.*

| Week | Theme | Key Activity | Design Concept |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **6** | **The "Juice" Phase** | **Sensory Feedback:** Adding screen shake, sound effects, and particle bursts. | Multi-sensory feedback and "Game Feel." |
| **7** | **The Reality Check** | **Peer Playtesting:** Students play each other's games and provide structured feedback. | Debugging, User Experience (UX), and Iteration. |
| **8** | **The Grand Showcase** | **The Designer's Log:** Presenting the game alongside the original paper prototype. | Presentation skills and "Post-Mortem" reflection. |

---

## 📝 Student Deliverables
1.  **The Designer’s Log:** A collection of paper prototypes, flowcharts, and sketches.
2.  **The Technical Build:** A hosted, playable digital game.
3.  **The Post-Mortem:** A short reflection on what worked, what failed, and how they would "Level Up" the game in version 2.0.

---

## 🚀 Technical Stack Recommendation
*   **Platform:** [Replit](https://replit.com) (Web-based, zero installation).
*   **Language:** Python.
*   **Library:** `Pygame` (for robust 2D game development).
